What is an empty leg flight?

A charter aircraft often flies one leg without passengers: returning to base after a one-way charter, or repositioning to collect its next client. The operator pays for fuel, crew, and handling either way, so the empty sector is offered at a reduced price.

The aircraft, crew, and service match a standard charter. The trade-offs: the route and departure time are fixed by the operator, and the flight cancels if the primary charter changes. Our guide to the most affordable way to charter a private jet compares the options, and the cost per hour breakdown shows what a standard charter runs.

How enquiries work here

Flights are compiled from charter operators' own published availability. Prices shown are the operator's published figure for the whole aircraft, not a per-seat fare, and they change without notice. Flight times are shown only where the operator published them.

Your enquiry goes through our quote form to operators covering the route, who confirm availability and final price with you directly.
