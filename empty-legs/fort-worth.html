Empty leg flights from Fort Worth

North Texas runs on corporate aviation, and Fort Worth is one of its centres. The empty leg flights that come off it are mostly short Texas hops created by aircraft repositioning between company bases.

The corridor that repeats most is Fort Worth to College Station and back, which has appeared in both directions. Austin and Abilene recur outbound, Atlanta inbound, and Aspen shows up when the ski season pulls traffic north.

The airports

  • Meacham International (FTW) — close to downtown and the field most Fort Worth listings mean.
  • Fort Worth Alliance (AFW) — north of the city, with long runways and plenty of hangar space.
  • Dallas Love (DAL) and Addison (ADS) — across the metroplex, often listed separately.
  • Dallas/Fort Worth International (DFW) — the airline field, used by the largest business aircraft.

The metroplex has more business aviation airports than almost anywhere in the United States, so check the field before you commit to a drive.

What an empty leg from Fort Worth costs

Where an operator publishes a price it is shown exactly as listed, as a whole-aircraft figure rather than a per-seat fare. Where none is published the card says so rather than estimating.

Texas sectors are usually under two hours and flown by light and midsize types. Common types include the Citation XLS, the Challenger 350 and the Legacy 500. You can also browse the whole list by aircraft type.
